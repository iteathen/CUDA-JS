# Assessment and Planning

## Proportionality

Routine reversible work may use a short inline assessment. Foundational, cross-component, native, security-sensitive, difficult-to-reverse, empirically uncertain, or cross-session work requires a durable assessment/plan.

## Assessment questions

Answer every applicable question:

- What exact outcome and non-goals apply?
- Which authority and specifications govern?
- What is known, assumed, proposed, or experimentally unresolved?
- Who owns each contract, resource, state transition, failure, and cleanup action?
- Which ranges, units, identities, capacities, and platform profiles are fundamental?
- What callers, dependencies, generated products, caches, artifacts, and external resources are affected?
- How can the strongest credible design fail?
- What cheaper or more universal path exists?
- What is the decisive falsifier or independent oracle?
- What rollback, safe stop, cleanup, compatibility, and handoff obligations exist?

## Plan shape

A sound plan states:

- exact input revision and authority;
- ownership-sized nodes with dependencies;
- outputs and write surfaces;
- acceptance and falsifiers;
- evidence and claim limits;
- invalidation conditions;
- rollback/safe stop;
- cleanup and integration;
- final review and handoff.

## Administrative restraint

Use one authoritative record by default. Link to existing authority and evidence rather than duplicating them. Do not create a ledger or form without a real consumer, owner, and lifecycle.
